A vocabulary for the argument everyone is already having

2026 · built solo, in public, at belleofthebot.com

133 explainer cards on AI, each one marked with what kind of claim it is and where it came from — and one source file that the website, the quizzes and 133 Instagram carousels all generate from.

overview

People argue about AI using words nobody has agreed on, and the arguments get worse because the same sentence can be a measurement, a definition, a theory, or one company’s stated position, and nothing on the page tells you which. That confusion is not a side effect. It is most of the problem.

So I built the thing I wanted to read: one idea per card, in plain language, with its source named and a mark saying what kind of claim it is. Then I made the whole site generate from a single Python file, so the browsable cards, three levels of gated quizzes, the sources page and 133 eight-slide Instagram carousels are the same content rendered four ways and cannot drift apart.

Static HTML, no framework, no build step in the browser, hosted free.

outcome

  • One source of truth, four outputs. Add a card once and it appears on the site, in the quiz bank, on the sources page and as a ready-to-post carousel. Nothing has to be kept in sync by hand, so nothing falls out of sync.
  • Every card admits what is unresolved. Each one carries a what we do not know panel, and the build fails if a card is missing it — because on this subject the confusion is the rule, not the exception.
  • Quizzes that teach rather than score. Three gated levels a subject, 80% to advance, and a wrong answer always names the right one and says why. The hardest level asks you to classify the claim yourself, which is the actual skill the site is for.
  • Claims corrected before publication, not after. Several widely repeated figures did not survive a look at the primary source and were changed or dropped.

honest limitation

I am not a researcher and nothing here is peer reviewed. The site’s whole claim is careful handling of other people’s evidence, which means the standard I have to meet is applying it to myself — and the first time I measured my own quizzes properly, they failed. The correct answer was the longest option on 91% of cards, so a reader could have scored full marks without reading a single question. Fixing that turned up three cards where the answer key was simply wrong. Both are fixed and both are on this page because leaving them off would be the exact failure the site is about.

the four marks, and why four4 marks

Every card carries exactly one, and the mark is the product. It is what a reader takes away even if they forget the card.

  • measurement — somebody measured this, and the card says who, when and on what sample.
  • theory — this is an argument, not an observation. Marking it must not read as dismissing it: arguments about things that have not happened yet are how they get prevented.
  • definition — this is what a word means, and the meaning may be contested.
  • someone’s position — this is what a named party says, and their interest in saying it is part of the card.

The fourth is the one that does the most work. A great deal of AI coverage is a company’s stated position wearing the grammar of a finding, and once you can see that, you cannot unsee it.

one file, four outputs1 source

A dictionary of card specs is the only place content lives. Two generators read it and write everything else:

  • the browsable site — 133 cards, filterable by subject and by kind of claim, each opening a seven-panel walkthrough.
  • the quizzes — five subjects, three levels each, plus a bonus round, all derived from the same questions the cards already carry.
  • the sources page — every citation on the site, in one list, generated rather than maintained.
  • the carousels — 133 eight-slide Instagram decks at 1080 × 1350, with captions and alt text, rendered from the same specs.

That is 1,064 slides that cannot say something different from the website, because there is nowhere for them to disagree. The alternative — a content file, a slide deck and a spreadsheet of sources — is three things to update and two that quietly go stale.

Guards run at import: the build refuses to start if any card is missing its unknowns panel, if any card’s answer key is in the wrong slot, or if a smiling illustration lands on a card about suicide or extinction.

the panel that says what nobody knows133 panels

An AI-literate friend reviewed an early version and objected that the cards read as more settled than the field is. He was right. The fix was not a disclaimer at the bottom of the site, which nobody reads, but a panel on every single card, one step before the source.

An analogy from that conversation is on the about page because I have not been able to improve on it: physics has a cutting edge of open questions wrapped around a very large body of settled understanding. This has a small area of understanding surrounded by questions we do not yet have the language to formulate.

So the cards are not a summary of what is known. They are a vocabulary for a conversation that is going to happen whether or not the vocabulary is ready.

the tell that only measurement found91% → 21%

Multiple choice has a well-known failure: the writer knows the answer, so the right option gets the caveats, the precision and the length, and the wrong ones get a few words. I knew about it. I did it anyway, on all 133 cards.

Measured: the correct answer was the single longest option 91% of the time, averaging 66 characters against 28 for the distractors. Picking the longest option scored full marks on every quiz on the site without reading a single question.

Rewriting all 532 options brought it to 21%, which is chance, with the correct answer’s length rank spread evenly across all four positions. The rewrite also surfaced three cards whose answer key had drifted into the wrong slot, so the site had been marking a correct answer wrong. There is now a guard that fails the build if it happens again.

The general lesson is the one the whole site is about: a quality you have not measured is a quality you are guessing at, and the guess flatters you.

figures that did not survive the source5 corrections

Every number on the site was traced to its primary source before publication. Several widely repeated ones did not hold:

  • A CEO’s much-quoted 25% is his estimate that things go really, really badly — not, as it is usually reported, a probability of extinction.
  • The bottle of water per email figure was revised roughly thirty-fold downward by its own author. The original still circulates.
  • A widely cited meta-analysis on AI and mental health was retracted, so it is not on the site at all.
  • A 2021 UN report frequently described as the first autonomous drone killing says targets were “remotely engaged” and attributes no death to a machine deciding by itself.
  • The best-known demonstration of gender bias in word embeddings is substantially a method artefact, which is a more interesting fact than the version everyone repeats.

None of these make the underlying concerns smaller. Getting them right is what makes the rest of the site worth believing.
