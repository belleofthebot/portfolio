Design rules that turn a month into an hour

2026 · the design system was already written down

A product catalog was quoted at a month of InDesign work. The first draft was finished before the meeting that assigned it ended.

what happened

the ask

A new product catalog. The room's working assumption was about a month — that being roughly what it takes to lay out a document like that in InDesign, page by page, with every spec table and product image placed by hand.

what I had

A design system already written down as rules a machine could follow, not as a PDF style guide nobody opens. Type scale, color roles, table treatments, image rules, the lot — set up in Claude Design as constraints rather than descriptions.

during the hour

I built the first draft while the meeting was still going. Not an outline — a real draft of the document, laid out against the actual rules.

after

It still needed review, correction and production work. A first draft is a first draft. But the starting line had moved by about four weeks.

why it worked, and it isn't the AI

The speed didn't come from the model. It came from the rules already existing in a form something else could apply.

Most design systems are documents about a design system — a PDF describing what the type scale is, which somebody has to read, remember and then execute by hand every time. That system produces consistency only as a side effect of somebody being careful. Written as constraints instead, the same system produces consistency as a property of the output.

The prompting is a real skill and I'd rather not pretend otherwise. Knowing how to specify a document so the result is usable rather than plausible-looking is the difference between an hour and a week of corrections. But the leverage was in the preparation. If the rules hadn't been written down first, the hour would have produced something that needed throwing away.

the same rules, on CambiumPak

I can't publish my employer's system, so here is the same idea on the invented company — the constraints that let any new CambiumPak document come out correct without a layout decision being made by hand.

Document rules

written as constraints, not descriptions

rulewhat it decides
type: one grotesque, two weightsHeadings 600, body 400. No display face — a mid-market manufacturer doesn't have one, and pretending otherwise breaks the character.
dimensions: internal, mm, L × W × DEvery dimension in every document, in that order. No conversions, no helpful inches.
moss = recycled figures onlyOne accent, one meaning. A moss-colored number that isn't a recycled percentage is a defect.
recycled content is banded0–39 / 40–69 / 70–100. Never implied, never a bare number where a band is the claim.
badges resolve from the recordCertification marks are looked up per SKU, never placed by hand — and a family row shows only what every member holds.
absent means absentA missing asset is removed, never substituted. A visible gap beats a wrong image.

Six rules, and five of them settle a question that would otherwise be re-decided on every page of every document. That's where the month goes: not into the layout, into the deciding.

what this says about how I work

  • I learn tools as they arrive rather than waiting to see whether they stick. The catalog was fast because I'd already put the work into a tool most of the room hadn't tried yet.
  • I'm not attached to the old way of doing it. I've spent years in InDesign. That's not a reason to spend a month in it.
  • The thing I gave back was time — the most expensive and least recoverable resource a small team has. Four weeks of a designer's month, returned to the schedule.

the honest version

A first draft is not a finished document, and I'd be overselling this if I let "an hour" stand without that. It needed review, factual checking against the source data, and production work before it went anywhere. What collapsed was the blank-page phase and the layout phase — which happened to be most of the month.
